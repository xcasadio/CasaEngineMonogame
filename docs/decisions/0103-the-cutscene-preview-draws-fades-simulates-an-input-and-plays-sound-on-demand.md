# ADR-0103: The cutscene preview draws screen fades, simulates an input and plays sound on demand

- **Status**: Accepted
- **Date**: 2026-11-19
- **Source**: this chantier: `ai-agent/tasks/cutscene-editor-fidelity-tasks.md` (decisions D1 to D5, settled with the author, and choices P1 to P15, taken by the agent and to be confirmed by the author at the acceptance), branch `chantier/cutscene-property-editing`. Completes ADR-0096, which deferred the drawing of fades, the sound button and the simulated input to later slices; it does not rewrite it.

## Context

- ADR-0096 decided that a cutscene is previewed in an isolated copy of a world, that sound is muted by default with a button to enable it, that screen fades are drawn over the preview, and that `ChangeWorld` and the chaining of `ExitOnInput` are only reported. The drawing of fades, the sound button and the simulated input were deferred (`docs/decisions/0096-the-cutscene-editor-previews-in-an-isolated-world-and-projects-the-tree-on-a-timeline.md`, section Consequences).
- In the game, the fade is drawn by `ScreenEffectComponent`: a one-pixel white quad stretched over the whole view, of the color of the `ScreenEffectService`, submitted through `SpriteRendererComponent.DrawSprite` with the blend of the service. Its blend states (additive, subtractive, the engine "opaque" with a `Max` alpha, non-premultiplied alpha) are private to `SpriteRendererComponent`, and the sprite shader discards a texel whose alpha, after the product by the color, is 0.01 or less (`CasaEngine/Framework/Application/Components/ScreenEffectComponent.cs`, `SpriteRendererComponent.cs`, `Content/Shaders/SpriteBatch.fx`).
- The preview viewport is a `WorldViewportPanel` with a preview world: its camera is not a `Camera2dComponent`, so the formula of the game does not apply. The engine offers `RenderView.RegisterPostUIOverlay(IPostUIOverlay, order)`, called after the interface of the view, and the editor already draws in 2D on a view with `SpriteBatch`. When shadows are active the target bound at the call is an intermediate scene target, so the size to cover is read from `graphicsDevice.Viewport`.
- The preview effect host (`CutscenePreviewEffectHost`) returns no audio service (`Audio` is `null`), refuses every game request and records it. Without an audio service the coroutine factory does nothing for `PlaySound`, `PlayMusic` and `StopMusic`, and `FadeMusic` does not wait for its duration. In the game, `ExitOnInput` arms a watch that replaces the previous one and falls at the end of the play.
- The editor has a single `AudioService` and a single mixer for the whole process (`AudioSystemComponent`), shared with the sound panel and the Play mode. `FadeMusic` writes the volume of the `Music` bus of that mixer; `StopMusic` and a cross-faded `PlayMusic` act on the shared music player; the voices started by a cutscene are owned by its world. The project may mute the `Master` bus (ADR-0040).
- The execution policy of the editor preview (`GameplayExecutionPolicy`, `EditorPreview`) freezes sprite animations, physics and scripts; it is carried by the editor game and common to all its worlds. Video played on a sprite already runs in the preview.
- No cutscene file of the repository uses `PlaySound`, `PlayMusic`, `StopMusic` or `FadeMusic`.

## Decision

- **Drawn fade (P1).** The fade is drawn in the image of the preview by an overlay registered on the render view of the preview viewport, through the `IPostUIOverlay` hook of the engine. It draws one rectangle over the whole view, with the color and the blend of the screen effect of the preview. The runtime is not modified.
- **Screen coordinates (P2).** The rectangle is drawn in screen coordinates with `SpriteBatch`, as an existing overlay of the editor does. The graphics device states changed for this draw are restored.
- **Blend states (P3).** The blend states are those of the engine, copied into the editor because they are private to the runtime: the engine opaque (color `One`/`Zero`, alpha `Max`), non-premultiplied alpha, additive and subtractive. A test compares the editor states field by field with those of the engine, so that a divergence shows. The veil is not drawn when its alpha is 2 or less, as the engine shader discards those texels.
- **Status line (P4).** The status line keeps its text mention of the fade ("Screen fade in progress", "Screen overlay rgba(...)"): it says what the image shows, which helps when the veil is faint.
- **Armed input (P5).** When a running play has passed an `ExitOnInput`, the preview holds it as armed, as the game does: the last `ExitOnInput` passed replaces the previous one, and the arm falls at the end of the play. One button per armed input, labelled `Press "<name>"`, appears under the transport bar; there is no button while nothing is armed.
- **Simulated input reports and stops (P6).** Pressing that button stops the play and the preview writes: `Input "<name>": the game would play <next cutscene>. The play ends here.`. The next cutscene is not played in the preview (D2). The state shown is "Ended", the world keeps its state, the playhead stays where it was.
- **Only while playing (P7).** The simulated input acts only during a running or paused play, not during a seek. It goes through no device: it is a button, not a key.
- **Sound on demand (P8).** A `Sound: off` / `Sound: on` button in the transport bar. It is off at each opening of a document and is not remembered, so that no preview makes noise unless asked.
- **A mute service keeps the durations of the game (P9).** Sound actions always have a service. The preview host owns a mute service (an `AudioService` on the engine mute device), recreated at each reset. It hands out the audio service of the editor only when the sound is on and the current step is a normal play step, that is a step the transport makes in `Running` mode. For any other step it hands out the mute service: a seek step, a frame step (`+1 frame`) and the zero-duration step of the first instant. Consequences: durations are always those of the game (`FadeMusic` waits for its duration in every case, which lifts the limit known since slice C); a seek replays exactly the same course as a play; nothing is heard during a seek, otherwise every sound on the way would start at once. A music started before the instant reached by a seek is not heard afterwards: `Restart` is needed.
- **The mixer of the editor is captured once and handed back (P10).** The state of the mixer of the editor is captured once per launch, right after the cleaning of the previous play, whether the sound is on or not; it is never captured when the button is switched on. When the preview has lent the editor service since that capture, it hands it back clean at each pause, `Stop`, seek, frame step, restart, end of play, switch off, change of preview world and closing of the document: it stops the voices owned by the preview world, stops the music, and applies the captured state back to the mixer (a `FadeMusic` writes the volume of the music bus, which is the bus of the whole editor). The loan stays noted until the next launch capture, so every stop point restores the same state, even if a fade begun before the switch-off kept writing in between. It is a stop, not a pause: resuming after a pause does not restart a music. The stop of the music acts on the music player shared by the editor.
- **Muted master bus (P11).** If the project mutes the master bus of the mixer, the sound stays inaudible even when on; the diagnostics and the documentation say so.
- **What stays frozen (P12).** Sprite animations, physics and scripts stay frozen. The execution policy is that of the editor game, common to all its worlds; changing it for the preview world alone needs a separate decision. Video on a sprite already plays. The mention of the status line does not change.
- **Automation (P13).** `--cutscene-preview-sound` switches the sound on before the play; `--cutscene-preview-input <name>` simulates the input after the other transport steps. With a play running, that step waits until the preview has made at least one step and an input is armed, for 120 editor frames at most; after that delay with nothing armed, or at once without a play, it is refused and noted. The diagnostics write the state of the veil (active, color, blend), the armed inputs, the simulated input and its target, and the state of the sound (button, service present, muted or not, the loan since the launch, and the count of normal play steps, of other steps and the nature of the last step).
- **No sound sample (P14).** No sample plays a sound and this decision adds none: a sound file would be one more binary file in the repository for a single trial. Sound is validated by tests with the fake device and by the ear of the author at the acceptance.
- **Runtime unchanged (P15).** The `CasaEngine/` runtime is not modified.

## Consequences

- A `FadeScreen` or `FadeScreenFrames` darkens, lightens or tints the preview image with the same blend as the game, at the instant of the playhead.
- The author can see which game input the preview has passed, and where the game would go, without leaving the editor; the next cutscene itself is still not played there.
- Sound can be heard during a normal play, and only then. Durations of music actions are those of the game in every case, since a service is always given.
- The audio service of the editor is shared: the preview hands it back clean at every stop point, which also cancels any mixer setting made elsewhere between the launch and that point. A music fade begun during an audible play keeps acting on the music volume of the editor during a switch-off, a seek or a frame step that follows, until the next stop point, where the state is handed back. The mute service writes a warning in the log for each sound it cannot play.
- The audible sound cannot be validated by a screenshot or a sample (P14); open point O1 of the plan stays until the author listens.
- Deferred: pausing and resuming sound, playing after a seek the music started before the reached instant, remembering the state of the sound button, a sound sample, a per-world execution policy, and playing in the preview the cutscene reached by an input or a world change.
