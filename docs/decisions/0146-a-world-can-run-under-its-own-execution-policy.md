# ADR-0146: A world can run under its own execution policy, and the cutscene preview runs sprite animations, particles and physics

- **Status**: Proposed
- **Date**: 2026-11-19
- **Source**: this chantier: `ai-agent/tasks/cutscene-editor-live-preview-tasks.md` (decisions D1 to D4 and choices P1 to P19), branch `chantier/cutscene-live-preview`. Builds on ADR-0135, ADR-0138 and ADR-0139. The number 0146 followed ADR-0145 of the camera chantier, which was not merged when this record was written; that record became ADR-0148 on 2026-11-20, when origin/main was merged.

## Context

- The execution policy is one per game. `GameplayExecutionPolicy` is a sealed class of ten required boolean properties (`CasaEngine/Framework/Application/GameplayExecutionPolicy.cs:3-23`) with three named instances, `Runtime`, `EditorPreview` and `EditorSimulation`. `CasaEngineGame.ExecutionPolicy` carries it and defaults to `Runtime` (`CasaEngine/Framework/Application/CasaEngineGame.cs:83`). The editor creates its runtime with `EditorPreview` (`CasaEngine.Editor/GameEditor.cs:1243`) and its Play mode switches the whole game to `EditorSimulation` and back (`CasaEngine.Editor/PlayMode/EditorPlaySessionController.cs:49`, `:73`). Nothing gives one world a policy of its own.
- The readers that concern one world read the policy of the game: `World.cs` (player controller initialisation, gameplay initialisation on load, `BeginPlay`, script update, script type resolution), `Entity.cs` (script update), `AnimatedSpriteComponent.cs` (`UpdateAnimatedSprites`), `PhysicsBaseComponent.cs` (`UseExternalViewManagement`, `UpdatePhysicsComponents`), `ParticleSystemComponent.cs` (`IsEditorPreview`) and `PhysicsSystemComponent.cs` (`UseExternalViewManagement`, `UpdatePhysicsEngine`). The policy is read during `World.LoadContent`, so a policy of a world must be set before that load.
- ADR-0135 made the cutscene editor preview in an isolated world, a copy of a project world, loaded into the runtime of the editor. ADR-0138 made the preview pause, step and seek by replay. ADR-0139 made the world live once no play runs. Under `EditorPreview` that world is still frozen for sprite animations, physics and scripts, and the preview status line says so (`CasaEngine.Editor/Controls/CutsceneAssetInspectorPanel.cs:37`). The documentation (`docs/editor/cutscene-editor.md:318`) calls changing the policy for the preview world alone a separate decision.
- Particles already simulate in the preview when `SimulateInEditor` is true, which is its default (`ParticleSystemComponent.cs:40`); their randomness is deterministic, from a fixed seed per emitter.
- `BepuPhysicsEngine` calls `OnHit` and `OnHitEnded` of the gameplay proxy of both entities without reading the execution policy. `AnimatedSpriteComponent` raises `AnimationFinished`, `AnimationLooped` and `AnimationEventTriggered` at five places, also without reading it.
- The physics engine advances by fixed steps of 1/60 s, one step per call (`PhysicsEngineSettings.cs:10`, `MaxSubSteps = 1`): a call that carries two steps or more performs one and loses the rest. In a game frame, the world is updated before the game components, among which the physics component, so an entity shows the pose of its body with one step of delay.
- The viewport of the preview is a view of the game of the editor. In the Play mode of the editor, the physics component of the game would advance the preview world as well as any other world it reaches.

## Decision

- **A world can carry its own policy (P1, P2).** `World` gains `ExecutionPolicyOverride` (null by default) and `ExecutionPolicy`, which returns the override when set and the policy of the game otherwise. It is null for a world with neither an override nor a game, and each reader keeps the tolerance it has today in that case. The override is set while the world has no game, so before `LoadContent`; setting it afterwards throws an `InvalidOperationException`. It is neither saved in a `.world` file nor copied with the world. The readers that concern one world read the policy of the world; the readers of `CasaEngineGame` and `GameManager` (window, views, first screen, virtual resolution) keep reading the policy of the game. Without an override, every world behaves exactly as today.
- **A fourth named policy, `GameplayExecutionPolicies.EditorLivePreview` (P3).** Like `EditorPreview`, except that `IsEditorPreview` is false (particles always simulate), `UpdateAnimatedSprites` and `UpdatePhysicsComponents` are true, and `RaiseGameplayNotifications` is false. `RunBeginPlay`, `UpdateGameplayScripts`, `InitializePlayerControllers` and `ThrowOnScriptTypeResolution` stay false.
- **Neither collisions nor animation events call game code in the preview (P4).** An eleventh boolean, `RaiseGameplayNotifications`, is true in the three existing policies and false in `EditorLivePreview`. It is read on the world of the entity in `BepuPhysicsEngine` before `OnHit` and `OnHitEnded`, and in `AnimatedSpriteComponent` before its three events. A world without a policy and an entity without a world notify as today. The collision lists of the components stay up to date. A new boolean is used rather than `UpdateGameplayScripts`, which is false under `EditorPreview`: reusing it would change what the worlds and the tests that step the physics under that policy do today.
- **`UpdatePhysicsEngine` becomes a per-world rule for the physics component of the game (P5).** When the game advances the physics, it skips a world whose policy says no, before asking for its physics context. In `EditorLivePreview` it is false, with the meaning "the game component does not advance this world, whoever drives it does". The Play mode of the editor therefore does not advance the preview world twice.
- **The preview advances its own physics, at the step of its transport (P6).** `PreviewWorldDriverOptions` gains `StepPhysics`, false by default so that the other previews do not change. With it, `Tick` calls `World.Update(seconds)` then `World.PhysicsWorld.Update(seconds)`, in the order of the game, and skips the physics when the step is zero or negative. The `TimeScale` of the game is not applied.
- **The preview world carries `EditorLivePreview` (P1, P3).** The factory given to `PreviewWorldDriver.Rebuild` sets the override on the copy of the world before its load. The status line of the preview says `Gameplay scripts are not updated in the preview.` (P9).
- **The world also lives outside a play (P7).** The rule of ADR-0139 does not change: the world stays live as soon as no play runs. After the `Stop` button, which already rebuilds the world, the start state is shown and then lives. `Play`, the `Stop` button, a move backwards and any seek started outside a play rebuild the world from its asset, as today, so the played state does not depend on the time spent stopped. Freezing the world outside a play is an alternative that is not retained and is to be judged at the author's acceptance test.
- **In the Play mode of the editor, the preview world keeps its own policy (P13).** Its scripts are no longer updated at that moment, as they would be today.
- **What a script already does in the preview does not change (P14).** Its initialisation, its `OnEndPlay` when the world is cleared and its `Draw` still run. The slice adds no call to a script: P4 closes the two paths that physics and animations would have opened.

## Consequences

- A world can be run under a policy different from the one of the game without a second game. Only the preview world of the cutscene editor uses it; the other previews of the editor and the world being edited receive no override.
- The preview shows sprite animations playing, particles simulating and physics advancing during a play, frozen by a pause and replayed by a move backwards. Gameplay scripts are still not updated.
- Three limits are accepted (P8):
  - (a) A play advances at the frame time of the editor and a seek by fixed steps of 1/60 s: the state of an animated sprite, of a particle or of a body at a given time may differ between a play and a seek.
  - (b) Only a seek that restarts the play (a move backwards, a modified document, no play running) gives a reproducible state: two such seeks to the same time give the same state. A forward seek during a play continues from the state of that play, as today, except from the marked start state, where it restarts.
  - (c) In a play, an editor frame that lasts two physics steps or more performs one, as in the game, so the physics falls behind the clock of the cutscene. The first step after a rebuild, bounded to 1/30 s, is in that case. A seek does not have this defect: each of its steps is one physics step.
- The skeletal animation of 3D models is not part of the slice (P15): its behaviour in the preview was not read and does not change.
- A seek costs more, since each replayed step also performs a physics step (P16). The cost is measured on the sample during the validation of the slice and written in the documentation; no cap is added.
- The new boolean is mandatory in `GameplayExecutionPolicy`, so any code that builds a policy with `new` must set it; the repository builds none outside the named instances.
- The slice that follows, the game view (world camera, virtual resolution, black bars, with a button back to the free view), has its own plan and is not decided here.
- The status of this record becomes `Accepted` once the author has validated the slice in the running editor.
